ISO 9001:2026 — TRANSITION TIMELINE

ISO 9001:2026 transition deadline: when is yours? It is probably not 2029.

The transition window runs about three years. Your certification body will transition you at your recertification audit, which for most organizations falls well inside it. Here is how to find your actual date, what to ask your CB, and what to do in the months before it.

The short answer

The official transition window for ISO 9001:2026 runs about three years from publication on September 16, 2026, so certificates to the 2015 edition are expected to expire around September 2029. That is the outside limit. It is almost never your deadline.

Your deadline is the first recertification audit that falls inside the window, because certification bodies transition you at recert. If your certificate expires in mid-2027, your recertification audit is in early 2027, and that audit is your transition audit. The three-year window shrinks to six months the moment you look at your own certificate.

The one-line version: find your certificate expiry date, subtract about three months, and that is when you will be audited against ISO 9001:2026.

How the window actually works

Three separate clocks are running, and people confuse them.

1. The publication and withdrawal date

ISO 9001:2026 was published on September 16, 2026. ISO 9001:2015 and its 2024 climate amendment were withdrawn the same day. From that date, the 2026 edition is the only current version of the standard. New certifications should go straight to it.

2. The IAF transition window

The International Accreditation Forum sets the transition period that accreditation bodies and certification bodies follow. For the 2015 edition it was three years; the same length is being applied to 2026. At the end of the window, every remaining 2015 certificate is invalid regardless of the expiry date printed on it.

3. Your certification body's own schedule

This is the clock that matters. A CB has to train and witness its auditors on the new edition before it can audit to it, and it has to decide when it will stop issuing 2015 certificates. Most CBs handle the transition at your recertification audit, because that is the audit where the full system is reviewed anyway. Some will transition you at a surveillance audit if you ask and if their auditors are ready. A few will run a dedicated transition audit for a fee.

In the 2015 transition, the shops that waited until the last year found that the CBs' schedules were full and the audit-day rates had gone up. The ones who asked early got the slots.

Work out your own deadline

Take your current certificate and do this in order.

  1. Find the expiry date. It is printed on the certificate. Three-year cycle from the last recertification.
  2. Subtract two to three months. Recertification audits are scheduled before expiry so the new certificate can be issued without a gap. That date is your recert audit.
  3. Is that date before September 2029? If yes, that audit is your transition audit. If no, your CB will transition you at a surveillance audit or a special audit before the window closes; ask them which.
  4. Subtract another six months. That is when your gap analysis, document control sweep, and first management review under the 2026 inputs need to be done, so you have an internal audit against the new edition on record before the CB arrives.

The people with the least time are not the ones with 2029 recerts. They are the ones with early-2027 recerts who read "three-year transition" and relaxed.

Five questions to ask your certification body in writing

Send these by email so you have the answers on record. They take the CB five minutes and they define your project.

  1. On what date will you stop issuing certificates to ISO 9001:2015?
  2. Will you transition us at a surveillance audit, or only at recertification?
  3. How many additional audit days will the transition add, and at what rate?
  4. Will you accept our current management review and internal audit as evidence, or must both be performed against the 2026 edition before the transition audit?
  5. What happens to our certificate if the transition audit raises a major nonconformity and the window has closed?

The fourth question is the one that catches people. Several CBs expect to see at least one management review with the revised 9.3.2 inputs and one internal audit against the new edition before they will sign off. That means the work has to be done a full cycle before the audit, not the month before.

Questions

When does ISO 9001:2015 expire?

The 2015 edition was withdrawn on September 16, 2026, when ISO 9001:2026 was published. Certificates to the 2015 edition remain valid through the transition window, expected to close around September 2029, after which they are invalid regardless of the printed expiry date.

Is the ISO 9001:2026 transition deadline 2029?

2029 is the end of the window, not your deadline. Certification bodies transition organizations at their recertification audit, so if your recert falls before the window closes, that audit is when you must meet the 2026 edition.

Can I transition at a surveillance audit instead of waiting for recertification?

Often yes, if your certification body's auditors are qualified on the new edition and you request it. Expect additional audit days. Ask your CB in writing.

Do I need a management review and internal audit against 2026 before the transition audit?

Many certification bodies expect both. Plan to hold a management review using the revised 9.3.2 inputs and complete an internal audit against the new edition at least one cycle before the transition audit.

How long does the transition take?

About six months for a mature single-site system, following a gap analysis, document control sweep, management review, and internal audit. Larger or multi-site systems should allow nine to twelve months.
