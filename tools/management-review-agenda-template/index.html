How to use it

  1. Assign an owner to each input on the agenda tab; they bring the data.
  2. Before the meeting, mark every measure better or worse than last time so the meeting spends its time on what moved.
  3. Run the meeting down the agenda. Every input gets a line in the minutes, even "no change".
  4. Record each decision as showed, concluded, decided, with an owner and a date.
  5. Check the carry-over count. Actions that appear three reviews running are the honest measure of the review.

The method

Inputs are information; outputs are decisions.

Management review is where the people who control resources are required — by the standard, once a cycle at minimum — to look at whether the quality system is working. Nowhere else does the standard put top management in a room with your data and oblige them to respond. That makes it the quality function's single annual shot at getting things resourced. Most quality managers spend it presenting slides.

Notice the asymmetry in the clause. Inputs are information; outputs are decisions. A review that ends with everyone informed and nothing decided has produced no outputs, whatever the minutes say. A management review template has to be built around that fact: every required input with a named owner who presents their own data — purchasing presents supplier performance, operations presents process performance — because people defend numbers they had to stand behind, and a meeting where the quality manager presents everything becomes a briefing with an audience.

Previous actions go first, deliberately. Opening with last cycle's actions establishes that actions from this meeting are tracked and will be asked about. And every number sits beside the same number from last period — "fourteen NCRs" prompts no reaction; "fourteen against nine" prompts a question, which is what you came for.

The diagnostic is the carry-over count. One or two actions carrying over is normal life. A third of the list carrying over every cycle means actions are being agreed in the room and not resourced afterwards — which is a finding about the review itself, and belongs on its own agenda.

Questions

Before you buy.

What has to be in a management review under ISO 9001:2026?

The inputs list was revised: changes in interested parties' needs is now its own item, and the effectiveness of actions on risks and on opportunities are listed separately. The template's agenda reflects the 2026 list.

How often should management review happen?

Annual is the floor and for most manufacturers too slow. A full review annually with a shorter mid-cycle check on objectives and actions is a defensible compromise — whatever you choose, write it down and hold to it.

Which Excel version do I need?

Excel 2016 or later on Windows or Mac, or Google Sheets. Every formula is standard: no macros, no add-ins, no external links. In Google Sheets use File → Import → Replace spreadsheet.
