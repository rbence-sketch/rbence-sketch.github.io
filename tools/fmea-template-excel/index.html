How this one is different

The arithmetic is not the hard part of an FMEA; agreeing what a 7 means is. This workbook carries rating anchors for severity, occurrence and detection that the team agrees before scoring, computes RPN with priority bands and a top-ten ranking, flags any mode where detection scores high regardless of RPN, because the failure nobody can see is worse than a bigger one everyone can, and carries each action to closure with its control level, owner, due date and status, so you re-score after you implement and the summary counts it as verified.

How to use it

  1. Agree the rating anchors on the scales tab before anyone scores a line.
  2. List each process step and its failure modes, effects and causes.
  3. Score severity, occurrence and detection against the anchors. RPN and the priority band compute.
  4. Read the top-ten ranking and the detection-gap flags first; those are the actions.
  5. Record the action, its control level, owner and due date on the same line; after you implement, re-score in the last columns and the summary counts it as verified.

The method

A usable FMEA template starts with anchors, not scores.

Every FMEA template multiplies three numbers. Severity, occurrence, detection; the product is the risk priority number. The spreadsheet part is trivial. The hard part of a failure mode and effects analysis is getting five people to mean the same thing by "a 7."

That is why a usable FMEA template starts with anchors, not scores. What does a severity of 9 mean here — a customer complaint, or a safety event? What occurrence counts as "likely" for this process — monthly, or on most runs? Score as a group, against written anchors, and score the process as it exists today — not as it will exist after your fix. The whole point is to find out which failures your fix still leaves exposed.

Then watch the detection score. A failure scoring 3/2/9 and one scoring 9/3/2 have the same RPN, and the first one is more dangerous — it is invisible until a customer finds it. When two items tie, fix the one with the higher detection score first. A good FMEA template flags that for you instead of sorting by RPN and calling it done.

1–10 for each of severity, occurrence and detection, the AIAG convention, with written anchors for each score so a team scores consistently.

What RPN needs action?

Thresholds are yours to set — the template ships with defaults and a rule that a high detection score outranks an equal RPN with a low one.
