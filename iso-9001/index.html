ISO 9001 — CLAUSE GUIDES

The clauses a quality manager runs every week, explained by an auditor

Five guides, one per clause, each with the requirement in plain words, what a certification auditor asks for, the findings that come up most, a method that works on a shop floor, and the free tool that runs it. Written to the 2026 edition, with the 2015 differences called out.

By Ryan Bence, ISO 9001 Lead Auditor · Updated September 26, 2026 · Clause numbers are pointers; nothing here reproduces the standard's text.

The guides

  • ISO 9001 Clause 9.2 Internal Audit: Requirements Explained. What ISO 9001 clause 9.2 requires of an internal audit program, what an auditor asks to see, the most common findings, and a free checklist to run one.
  • ISO 9001 Clause 9.3 Management Review: Inputs and Results. What ISO 9001 clause 9.3 requires: the inputs management review must cover, the results it must produce, what an auditor checks, and a free agenda.
  • ISO 9001 Clause 10.2 Nonconformity and Corrective Action. What ISO 9001 clause 10.2 requires when a nonconformity occurs, the two records to keep, what an auditor checks, and a free root cause template.
  • ISO 9001 Clause 8.4 External Providers: Supplier Control. What ISO 9001 clause 8.4 requires for suppliers and outsourced processes: evaluation, selection, monitoring, re-evaluation, and a free scorecard.
  • ISO 9001 Clause 7.5 Documented Information Explained. What ISO 9001 clause 7.5 requires for documents and records: what must exist, how it is controlled, what an auditor checks, and the 2026 wording change.

The 2026 edition

ISO 9001:2026 was published on September 16, 2026. Everything that changed, clause by clause, is on the what changed page, with a free readiness readout and spokes on the transition deadline, the document control sweep, clause 10, quality culture and risks and opportunities.
